## What she has written

Three posts, all in English.

### "Handling mixed languages in small models" (March 2025)

An experiment on whether prompt technique can keep a small model answering in
the target language when the retrieved context is in a different one.

- Five techniques compared: no technique; an instruction to answer in the same
  language as the user; the language as a section at the start of the prompt;
  the language as a constraint; the language as a directive appended to the user
  message.
- Three models: llama3.2:3b, qwen2.5:3b, qwen:4b. Context language English,
  target language Portuguese. Mixed-language context at 0%, 50% and 100%. 1000
  trials per condition. Context sampled from Wikimedia; language detection by
  gpt-4o-mini.
- Results: llama3.2:3b held up almost perfectly throughout, even against fully
  English context. qwen2.5:3b needed explicit guidance to stay high. qwen:4b
  degraded badly at 100% English context, landing between 55% and 75%.
- Appended directive was the strongest technique overall, then constraint.
  "Answer in the same language as the user" was the least reliable of all when
  the context language disagreed.
- The scenario that motivates the whole piece: a Portuguese-speaking user asking
  an e-commerce support chatbot about return policies, while the retrieved
  documents are in English, and the chatbot answering in a confusing mix.
- Portuguese appears in this post as an experimental target language and in that
  scenario. It is not a language she claims to speak.

### "Voice agent without the bloat" (April 2025)

A deliberately lean voice agent, built to argue that complexity is not what
makes these things work.

- Vanilla JS with the browser's native MediaRecorder, a hold-to-talk button, a
  mouth animated in pure CSS, Vite for the build.
- Hono on Node for the backend, containerized with Docker, hosted on Render.
- Direct calls to OpenAI for STT, chat completion and TTS, with no LangChain or
  other intermediary library in between.
- Rhubarb Lip Sync, open source and free, generates the mouth cues from the
  final TTS audio.
- Chose hold-to-talk over real-time streaming on purpose, trading latency for
  simplicity and cost: no WebRTC, no persistent connections, no barge-in.
- The demo agent's persona is "The Alchimist of the Electronic Ether", which
  answers cryptically, partly so a public URL does not become a free
  general-purpose assistant.
- Live at voice-agent-front.onrender.com.
- Closes on subtraction: the art is in how much you can remove while still
  delivering value.

### "The hidden cost of an autonomous assistant" (July 2026)

On self-hosting Nous Research's Hermes agent, and on the category around it
(OpenHands, Goose, Devin). Her verdict up front is that this is not a finished
product, it is an MVP-grade experiment.

- The genuine upside: frontier open models have arrived, and she points at Kimi
  K3, a 2.8T-parameter MoE with a 1M-token context window and native vision,
  whose weights Moonshot published openly in July 2026. Plus self-hosted
  ownership, persistent memory on disk you control, self-authored skills, and
  24+ messaging gateways.
- The one reason to pay for it: configurability. Any model, any provider, your
  own storage, your own interaction model, and you can edit the execution code
  itself.
- The costs: every service is separate plumbing you assemble by hand (model
  provider, an email identity, a phone number on its own SIM for WhatsApp, a
  Google account, per-channel gateway config); you are now running and backing
  up a VPS at roughly $5 to $10 a month that holds the agent's entire memory;
  you need a provider like Fastmail because outbound mail from a datacenter IP
  looks exactly like abuse to Google; the Google Cloud setup is the one step
  easier than feared, since `gws auth setup` provisions the project for you; and
  configuration is terminal-first, YAML over SSH.
- The risks: VPS IPs are static and get fingerprinted, so Google can lock the
  account even through the official CLI, and WhatsApp bans numbers that behave
  like bots. Auto-installed and self-authored skills are just code running on
  your box with your agent's credentials, unreviewed. And these agents are not
  hardened to talk to strangers, so exposing their channels is opening a breach.
- Where it fits today: internal back-office automation and tooling, for you and
  a tightly scoped allow-list of trusted identities. Not in front of customers.
  She estimates months of engineering-team work to get one safe enough for end
  users.

## Labs

Two WebGL proofs of concept on the site, both labelled as experiments.

- **Liquid page**: the whole rendered DOM, navbar included, is captured into an
  image through an SVG foreignObject with html-to-image, used as a texture on a
  fullscreen Three.js quad, and distorted per pixel in a fragment shader (twist,
  swirl, smear and ripple around the cursor). The real DOM stays underneath at
  opacity 0 and still interactive, so what you click is the invisible page and
  what you see is the shader. Stated caveats: the capture is a static snapshot
  so hover states do not repaint, and it stays off under
  prefers-reduced-motion.
- **Liquid background**: the same distortion effect applied to the background
  only.
